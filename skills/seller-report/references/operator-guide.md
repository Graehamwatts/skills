# The weekly seller report: a one-page guide for Adrian

Claude builds each listing's weekly seller report from the View Activity sheet you already keep. You do not need to learn anything new. This page says what to hand over, how to write a follow-up so it is read correctly, and which extra columns help if you want to add them.

## Each Monday

1. **Say which listing, and that the sheet is up to date through Sunday.** "Run the weekly for Sample Glen, the sheet is current" is enough. Claude opens the live sheet itself.
2. **Mention anything that is not on the sheet:** an offer that came in, a call you took on your cell, a forwarded email. Paste it or forward it, with the date. Someone who is not on the sheet at all needs a row first, or they will drop out of next week's count.
3. **Say if you want an extra this week:** online views from Zillow, similar homes nearby, the online ads status, a photo. None of them is needed.

You get back an unsent Gmail draft, addressed to the seller with you on CC, plus a few short "worth a glance" lines in the chat. Read the draft like any email. If something is wrong, say it in your own words ("Jordan never said it was overpriced", "take out the part about the neighbour", "I answered him myself on Oct 9") and the draft is fixed. The fix holds in every later week. Graeham sends it.

## Writing a follow-up so it reads correctly

- **One row per person, and keep adding to that row.** Do not start a new row for the same agent.
- **Start every note with its date,** then the note: `10/7 - called, no answer, left a vm. sent a text`. Each dated line is one entry. A line with no date in front of it belongs to the line above it.
- **Put their answer on its own line starting with `>`:** `> Thanks, her clients are still deciding.` That is how the report knows it is what they said, not what we did.
- **Say who called whom** when they called us: `10/8 - agent called us back, buyers liked the yard`. A bare "spoke with her" cannot be counted as our follow-up.
- **Write what they said about price plainly,** even when it is blunt: "said it's overpriced", "said the price is fair". The report softens the wording for the seller and keeps the meaning. Rude words about the home never reach the seller.
- **Mark a tour that did not happen.** "Cancelled" or "rescheduled" keeps it from being counted as a showing.
- **Phone numbers, lockbox codes and private details in the notes are fine.** They never reach the report. Still, leave out a buyer's health, family or money matters when you can.

## The Email, Call and Text columns

Keep putting the date in them the way you do now. One question is still open: does a date there mean the day the team made that call, text or email, the day someone typed the row in, or sometimes the day the agent called us? And when you add a new date, does it replace the old one? Until you answer, the report reads a date there as a follow-up our team made that day, unless the note says the agent called us. If that changes a number, the chat lines say so.

## Optional columns, if you want to add them

Add any of these to a tab as a new column. The report reads them when they are there and works without them. A value in one of your columns always wins over Claude's own reading of the notes.

| Column heading (any of these words works) | What to type |
|---|---|
| Price reaction | too high, fair, not raised |
| Interest | hot, warm, cold, passed |
| Offer intent | yes, maybe, no |
| Next follow-up date | the date, like 10/14 |
| Visitor count (open house tab) | a number, in the host's own unit: groups or visitors |

## Why the numbers can look lower than your hand-written reports

Your reports counted cancelled tours, questions and open house hosts. This report counts a showing only when a tour took place, counts a question as an inquiry, and counts hosts as part of our team, not as contacts. Same people, stricter counting, so the totals run a little lower. If a seller asks, that is the answer.
