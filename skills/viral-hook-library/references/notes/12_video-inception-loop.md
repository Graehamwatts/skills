A guy SPRINTS down the center aisle of a huge empty auditorium straight at the camera, phone in hand, face ecstatic — motion blur, handheld urgency. The implied question ("what is he so excited to show?") is the hook; the caption doubles it: "Have you seen this video?!"

## STRUCTURE
- 0-3s: Wide of the empty venue; tiny figure by the stage starts running up the aisle toward camera, arriving in a blurred close-up.
- 3-6s: HARD CUT to POV: hands holding an iPhone playing a video — in it, ANOTHER person sits in the same auditorium watching a phone.
- 6-14s: Recursion engine: each beat pushes INTO the on-screen phone, revealing another phone-in-phone layer (camera roll UI, scrub bars and timestamps visibly stacked 3-5 deep). Every layer is a different person being shown "the video."

None. All context sits in the caption (which converts the gag into service-time promo). The visible iPhone UI chrome (Today 1:16 PM, scrub bars) functions as accidental "text" that sells the nesting depth.

## TRANSITIONS & EDIT RHYTHM
Signature transition = RECURSIVE MATCH CUT: cut from a person reacting → POV of the phone they're holding, whose content contains the next person, then a slow push-in until that phone's screen fills frame, then repeat. Each nesting level ~2-3s, accelerating slightly as layers stack. One hard cut (run → first POV); everything after is push-in + screen-swallow. Feels like an infinite zoom without any digital zoom trickery.

## AI vs REAL
All real, shot in sequence: they filmed layer N on a phone, then filmed someone watching that recording, then filmed someone watching THAT, etc. Zero VFX — the "effect" is production sequencing, not editing. Slight screen glare/moire on each phone actually authenticates it.

The recursion also rewards rewatching (people loop it to count layers), juicing watch time. Institutional account using pure play, no message until the caption.
