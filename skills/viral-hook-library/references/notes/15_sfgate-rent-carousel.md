Just a few years ago, San Francisco was grappling with soaring office vacancies… The median rent for a one-bedroom apartment in San Francisco has reached an eye-watering $4,000 — up 21.2% from a year ago."

## HOOK (first 1-3s)
Slide 1: postcard shot of Alamo Square's Painted Ladies with downtown skyline, white serif headline top-left: "Rent in SF's Alamo Square is up 42%." + kicker "It signals a larger trend. →". A shocking local stat over an instantly recognizable landmark, with an explicit swipe arrow. Data = the hook.

## STRUCTURE
- S1: Stat hook (Alamo Square +42%) over Painted Ladies.
- S2: Context: "As AI companies expand their footprint in SF, the effects are showing up in the housing market" over rooftops/skyline.
- S3: Data: SoMa 1BR rents +36% YoY (Zumper), Mission Bay +22%, South Beach +21% — Sutro Tower vista.
- S4: Expert quote: biggest increases in nearby residential neighborhoods (housing expert Crystal Chen) — sidewalk cafe scene.
- S5: Neighborhood data barrage: Alamo Sq +42%, Cole Valley +39%, Inner Sunset +32%, Noe Valley +36% — mural patio photo.
- S6: "The same ripple effect is now spreading across the Bay Area" — freeway shot that happens to include an AI billboard ("Put AI to work for people").
- S7/8: End card: aerial Bay shot + SFGATE logo + "Visit the link in bio for more."

## TEXT OVERLAYS
Editorial style: white SERIF headline text, upper-left/top of frame, 2-4 lines max per slide, no pills or highlights — reads like a magazine pull-quote laid over full-bleed photography. Consistent placement slide-to-slide; the arrow glyph on slide 1 is the only "UI" element.

## TRANSITIONS & EDIT RHYTHM
Swipe-paced storytelling: one idea per slide, escalating from a single stat → cause → data → expert voice → more data → zoom out (region-wide) → CTA. Classic news-nut-graf structure ported to carousel. Photos alternate landmark/street-level for visual rhythm.

## AI vs REAL
All real editorial photography (street scenes, aerials). No AI imagery — the story is ABOUT AI money, told with human-scale photos.

## CONCEPT / WHY IT WORKS
Authoritative data journalism packaged for swiping: each slide pays off the previous one's open loop, and hyper-local neighborhood names (Cole Valley, Noe, Inner Sunset) trigger recognition and comment-section debate from locals. The caption adds the jaw-dropper (sellers accepting Anthropic/OpenAI stock) that the slides don't even need.
