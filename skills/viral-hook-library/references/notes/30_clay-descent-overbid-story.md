## HOOK (first 1-3s)
A caption is already on screen at frame 0 and the picture is already moving. The picture is a tilt-shift aerial of a dense city grid built like a beige clay model, every roof the same tan. The words build one to three at a time into a question: what makes a San Francisco buyer pay over asking price. No face, no logo, no address, no number.

## STRUCTURE
- 0-8s: One shot that looks unbroken (samples every 1 to 2 seconds show a single continuous fly-in), so no visible cuts. The camera flies in and down through the monochrome clay city and lands front-on to one house. That house is the only thing in full color, so the eye knows where to look before anyone names it. New caption roughly every second.
- ~8-21s: Hard cut to a photoreal rendered rooftop terrace with the skyline behind it. A camera-viewfinder "REC" overlay with a running timecode sits on the first beat only. A man works on a laptop, closes it, picks up a coffee, stands at the rail. The narration is second person ("closing your laptop", "to your afternoon") and ends by asking whether a terrace like this makes your must-have list.
- ~21-22s: Rendered exterior of the house with two labeled callout plates on leader lines (views, dedicated parking). Caption: "NON NEGOTIABLE".
- ~22-23.5s: Cutaway cross-section render of the whole house with four callout plates (in-unit laundry, dedicated storage, seismic retrofit and solid foundation, plus one at the frame edge I could not read).
- ~23.5-25s: Black card, white line-art logo, caption "FOLLOW US FOR".
- Five scenes in 25 seconds. No price, overbid figure or days on market appears in any sampled frame.

## TEXT OVERLAYS
- Narration captions are T3 karaoke caps: one to three words, extra-bold italic, white with a dark outline, one yellow word per line, popping with the voice.
- Feature callouts are off-white serif label plates with a dot and a leader line pointing at the thing named. Two text jobs, two looks: captions carry the voice, plates carry the facts.
- A lower-third disclaimer runs the whole video. Most of the run it reads "Video does not represent actual property as is." During the house descent it reads "We did not represent the buyer or seller in this transaction."
- Position: captions sit about three quarters of the way down the frame. That is inside Instagram's bottom 35% band, where the app's own caption and buttons sit. Copy the style, not the position. The disclaimers sit lower still.

## TRANSITIONS & EDIT RHYTHM
One apparently continuous camera move for about 8 seconds, one hard cut into a single 12-second scene, then three quick swaps in the last four seconds. The opening breaks our 3-cuts-in-5-seconds hook rule (hook_check would flag it). What replaces the cuts: fast camera motion, a new word on screen every second, and a subject that is color-isolated from the first frame. Treat it as an exception for continuous-flight hooks, not a new rule.

## AI vs REAL
Everything is generated. No real footage of the house or the block. The clay city is a restyled aerial, the terrace and the man are photoreal renders with small animated moves (typing, sipping, turning), and the callout plates and cutaway are graphics laid on renders. The disclaimer is the only thing standing between this and a misleading ad. The playbook rule that "the listing itself must be real footage" does not apply, because this sells a story and the brand, not the house. It is the fallback lane for when real footage is not available.

## CONCEPT / WHY IT WORKS
An open loop, a color-isolated subject, and a daydream. The question is asked in the first four seconds and never answered inside the video, so the only way to find out is the link. The one saturated house in a desaturated world does the H2 job (color-isolate the prop) with no prop. The terrace scene sells a feeling, you at 4pm with the laptop closed, instead of square footage. The feature stack then hands the viewer a rational excuse for agreeing with the overbid.

## WHAT IT LEAVES ON THE TABLE
The loop never closes in the video: no asking price, no sold price, no days on market. Captions and disclaimers both sit low. The disclaimer can be covered by Instagram's own caption text. There is a link-in-bio CTA and no comment keyword.

## REPLICATE FOR A LISTING OR A SOLD HOME (the no-assets version)
Use when we have no photos, no listing data we can show, or no permission from the listing agent.
1. Question in captions on frame 0, second person, with the number or the contrast inside the question ("why did buyers pay $X over asking?").
2. Clay-city descent. Start from a public map or satellite view of the block, restyle it to a beige clay look (Nano Banana Pro for the still, Seedance or Higgsfield to animate the fly-in), and leave only the subject house in color. The real block can appear. Nothing invented gets rendered onto the real house.
3. One lifestyle scene built from the single hero feature in the listing facts (terrace, view, yard). The person is a generic stand-in for the viewer, not Graeham.
4. Feature stack of three to five callout plates, each one a verified listing fact. Nothing rendered that the listing does not have.
5. The number card the source skips: asking price, sold price, days on market, percent over. Two to three seconds, inside the safe zone. This closes the loop the hook opened.
6. CTA is a comment keyword armed in GHL before posting (Part 6), not link in bio. End card carries the brokerage, Graeham's full name and his DRE number exactly as `skills/shared-references/identity.json` and the Compass advertising handbook require.
7. Disclaimer for the whole runtime, placed above the bottom 35% band and repeated in the caption text: the video is a rendering, not the actual property. Add "we did not represent the buyer or seller" only when that is true. For someone else's active listing, get the listing agent's OK first. Closed sales are the safer version of this format.
- Tier A for the descent and the callouts, Tier B for the terrace character. Difficulty 3/5 the first time (getting one consistent clay look and one consistent character), about 2/5 once the template exists.
- Fair housing: the lifestyle scene describes how the space is used, never who should live there.
